Where the weight goes

Take-off weight is the sum of crew, payload, fuel and the empty aircraft. Empty weight grows with take-off weight, so the solver iterates until the two agree.

W₀ = (W_crew + W_payload) / (1 − W_f/W₀ − W_e/W₀)

Method notes

Weight sizing

Payload is passengers plus cargo. Crew is two pilots plus one cabin attendant per 50 passengers, 90 kg each. The empty-weight fraction uses Raymer's statistical fit for jet transports, W_e/W₀ = 0.97 · W₀^−0.06 · K with W₀ in kg, where K is the structure technology factor.

Fuel

Take-off, climb and landing use Raymer's historical fractions (0.970, 0.985, 0.995). Cruise uses the Breguet range equation with cruise L/D = 0.866 · (L/D)max, the best-range point for a jet. The reserve loiter uses the endurance equation at (L/D)max. Total fuel adds 6% for reserves and trapped fuel: W_f/W₀ = 1.06 · (1 − ΠW_i/W_i−1).

Aerodynamics

A parabolic drag polar C_D = C_D0 + C_L² / (π·A·e) gives (L/D)max = ½·√(π·A·e / C_D0). This is how wing geometry feeds directly into fuel weight.

Constraints

Take-off: Loftin/Roskam FAR 25 correlation TOFL[ft] = 37.5 · (W/S)[lb/ft²] / (σ·C_Lmax,TO·T/W). Landing: LFL[ft] = 0.3 · V_app²[kt] with V_app = 1.23 V_stall, converted to take-off wing loading with the landing weight ratio. Cruise: thrust equals drag at cruise altitude, corrected by thrust lapse and the weight at start of cruise. Second-segment climb with one engine out: T/W = N/(N−1) · (1/(L/D)_TO + G), G = 2.4%, 2.7% or 3.0% for 2, 3 or 4 engines.

Limits

This is a first-pass (Class I) estimate. It does not size the fuselage, tail or landing gear, and it ignores compressibility drag rise, engine sizing effects on drag, and certification details. Its purpose is to show how the requirements couple and to give a starting point for detailed (Class II) design.

References: D. P. Raymer, Aircraft Design: A Conceptual Approach, AIAA. J. Roskam, Airplane Design, Part I. L. K. Loftin, Subsonic Aircraft: Evolution and the Matching of Size to Performance, NASA RP-1060.
