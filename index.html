2Weight estimate

The take-off weight is the empty aircraft plus fuel, payload and crew. The tricky part is that a heavier aircraft also needs a heavier structure, so the calculation is repeated until the numbers stop changing.

W₀ = (W_crew + W_payload) / (1 − W_fuel/W₀ − W_empty/W₀)

3Wing and engine size

Each line is one requirement: take-off, cruise, climbing with one engine failed, and landing. The shaded area shows designs that meet all of them. The design point is the smallest wing and the smallest engines that still work.

4Compare with real aircraft

A simple method like this is usually within about 10–20% of the real aircraft. The real aircraft also fly slightly different missions.

What I learned

  • Everything in aircraft design is connected. More passengers means more weight, which needs more fuel and a bigger wing, which adds even more weight.
  • Range and engine fuel consumption have a big effect on take-off weight. For long-haul jets, fuel is almost half of the total weight.
  • The constraint diagram shows which requirement decides the engine size. Here it is usually the take-off runway length.

This is a first estimate only. It does not design the fuselage, tail or landing gear. Methods from D. P. Raymer, Aircraft Design: A Conceptual Approach, and J. Roskam, Airplane Design, Part I.
